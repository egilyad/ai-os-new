APPLICATION  
“Adaptive Autonomous Installation for Producing Fresh Water from Atmospheric Air”

1. Technical Field  
The invention relates to systems for producing fresh water from atmospheric air.  
In particular, the invention relates to autonomous and partially autonomous installations that use renewable energy sources to intake, condition, cool, and condense atmospheric air, followed by collection and purification of the resulting condensate.  
The installation can be used in coastal cities, remote areas, residential and industrial buildings, agricultural facilities, and autonomous water-supply systems.

2. Background Art  
Atmospheric water generators are known in which water vapor from atmospheric air is condensed by cooling the air below its dew point temperature.  
Systems are also known that use solar energy, wind energy, refrigeration machines, heat exchangers, and various energy-storage systems.  
However, the efficiency of water production substantially depends on the temperature and relative humidity of the atmospheric air, as well as on the amount of available energy.  
At high ambient air temperatures, cooling large volumes of air requires a significant amount of energy.  
Moreover, the availability of solar and wind energy varies over time.  
Therefore, there is a need for an installation capable of automatically changing its water-production mode depending simultaneously on atmospheric conditions and available renewable energy. [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32642-0)

3. Objective of the Invention  
The objective of the invention is to create an installation for producing water from atmospheric air that:  
- uses more than one renewable energy source;  
- allows accumulation of available energy;  
- determines the temperature and relative humidity of the air;  
- determines or calculates the dew point;  
- adapts the airflow and cooling mode;  
- uses pre-cooling;  
- provides thermal energy recovery;  
- automatically distributes available energy among the functional units of the installation.

4. Technical Result  
The technical result is an increase in the efficiency of using available energy for producing water from atmospheric air through coordinated control of:  
- airflow;  
- cooling temperature;  
- condensation;  
- solar energy;  
- wind energy;  
- an electrical storage device;  
- a thermal storage device;  
- a heat-recovery system.  
An additional technical result is the ability to adapt the installation’s productivity to changing atmospheric conditions. [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32642-0)

5. Summary of the Invention  
The installation comprises an air duct, an air-moving device, an air pre-treatment system, a recuperative heat exchanger, a pre-cooling unit, and a condensation unit.  
Atmospheric air enters through an air intake and a filter, after which it is moved by a fan through the air duct.  
In the recuperative heat exchanger, heat exchange is carried out between air streams.  
The air then enters the pre-cooling unit and subsequently the condensation unit.  
In the condensation unit, the air temperature is reduced to a level at which water vapor condenses.  
The condensate is collected, purified, and directed to a clean-water reservoir. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10073925/)

6. Energy System  
The energy system comprises at least two renewable energy sources.  
In a preferred embodiment:  
10 — solar photovoltaic panels;  
12 — a wind turbine.  
The generated electrical energy is supplied to a controller/power converter 13.  
From there, energy is directed to the installation’s consumers and may be stored in a battery 14.  
Additionally, a solar thermal collector 11 is provided, connected to a thermal storage device 15. [pubs.acs](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsestengg.4c00098)

7. Adaptive Control System  
The installation includes:  
16 — a temperature sensor;  
17 — a relative humidity sensor;  
18 — an airflow and/or wind speed sensor;  
19 — an adaptive controller.  
The controller receives information from the sensors and determines the installation’s operating parameters.  
Based on the temperature and relative humidity, the dew point is determined or calculated.  
Thereafter, the controller adjusts:  
- fan speed;  
- pre-cooling capacity;  
- condensation-unit mode;  
- distribution of electrical energy;  
- use of the battery;  
- use of available solar and wind energy.  
Thus, the installation does not operate in a single fixed mode but selects an operating mode in accordance with current conditions. [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32642-0)

8. Heat Recovery  
The system comprises a heat-recovery device 20.  
Thermal energy removed during cooling and condensation can be partially reused.  
Recovered energy can be directed to:  
- pre-treatment of air;  
- preheating of individual streams;  
- the thermal storage device;  
- regeneration of installation components. [mdpi](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/17/22/10309)

9. Water Production  
After the condensation unit 6, the resulting condensate flows into a collector 7.  
The water then passes through a purification unit 8.  
After purification, the water enters the reservoir 9.  
Depending on the intended use of the installation, the purification unit may include:  
- mechanical filtration;  
- sorption purification;  
- a carbon filter;  
- membrane purification;  
- ultraviolet disinfection;  
- mineralization.

13. Abstract  
An adaptive autonomous installation for producing water from atmospheric air comprises an air duct, an air-moving device, a recuperative heat exchanger, a pre-cooling unit, a condensation unit, a condensate collector, a water purification system, and a reservoir. The energy system comprises at least two renewable energy sources, preferably photovoltaic solar panels and a wind turbine, as well as an energy storage device. The control system receives data on temperature, relative humidity, and airflow speed, determines or calculates the dew point, and selects an operating mode from a plurality of modes by regulating airflow and the condensation-unit mode taking into account the dew point and available energy capacity. A heat-recovery system is additionally provided, allowing thermal energy to be reused. The installation is intended for autonomous or partially autonomous production of fresh water from atmospheric air. [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32642-0)

Optional embodiment for coastal/marine air (add to Description, not to claims, to keep scope broad)  
In a variant adapted for humid marine air in coastal cities, the air intake (1) and filter (2) are configured to reduce salt-aerosol ingress. The intake includes a labyrinthine baffle and a multi-stage filtration train (e.g., a G4 pre-filter followed by an electrostatic or high-efficiency particulate filter). Air duct surfaces and heat-exchanger fins are coated with anti-corrosion, hydrophilic coatings; metallic components are made of stainless steel 316L or duplex steel, or aluminum alloys with marine-grade protection. Electronics are sealed to at least IP65. A conductivity sensor in the condensate collector (7) detects elevated salinity; upon detection, the control system (19) diverts the initial condensate fraction to drain and increases the share of recirculated air through the recuperative heat exchanger (4) to limit salt carryover. In high-salt conditions, the controller reduces fan speed to decrease aerosol loading and prioritizes energy to pre-filtration and purification stages. This variant improves reliability and water quality in coastal environments without limiting the general scope of the claims. [pubs.acs](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsestengg.4c00098)
